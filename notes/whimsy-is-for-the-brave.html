Whimsy is for the Brave

← the field notes
crystal st. john june 2026 philosophy / creative life

I was six years old, playing goalie at a soccer game my parents had come to watch, when I looked down and saw a dandelion. I immediately got down on the ground to inspect it.

Just then, the ball flew past me into the net. I looked up to find my parents' faces. Let's just say, they weren't clapping. That was the first time I realized who I actually was didn't quite fit into the constructs built for me.

I was the whimsical child. Not one to depend on.

I've spent most of my life trying to become someone dependable. Being dependable looks like doing the things I say I'll do, and finishing the projects I start. Except, all of it is done with whimsy. Herein lies Faeblette.

✦ ✦ ✦

Most people hear the word whimsical and picture mushrooms, a girl running through a field of daisies, or leaves dancing in the wind. That's how it gets painted. Light. Decorative. Soft at the edges.

Let me reframe it.

Whimsy is more than an aesthetic, it is a posture: a resistance to going numb, a refusal to sleepwalk through the hours, to stop noticing.

It is a call to presence, with all of it. The beauty and the pain. The dandelion and the missed goal.

Whimsy is a poetic lens. It is the choice to take the scenic route, to light the candle today rather than save it for some occasion that never comes, to have a romantic moment with your morning coffee, to see, really see, what's in front of you. It is the understanding that the dream is not somewhere out yonder. It is here. In the noticing.

✦ ✦ ✦

And presence, I've come to find, is where everything begins. Regulation. Health. Gratitude. The spiritual life.

The more spiritually attuned people become, the more whimsical their lives tend to bend. Because presence is the doorway to the sacred.

And whimsy, a way of honoring what's in front of you, first with acceptance and then with gratitude, is the aesthetic language of presence.

So maybe it isn't childish to pursue whimsy.

Maybe it's the truest act of devotion to this life that we've been given.

Xx Crystal
