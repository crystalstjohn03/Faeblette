on the creative process

bad work is part of the good work

← the field notes
crystal st. john may 2026 film / process

The Exposing Chair, my recently released short film, explores a question I have come back to over and over in my years of adulting.

When do we wake up and know what we're doing? I used to look at people who had accomplished something with their lives and wonder: how did they bridge the gap? How did they go from not knowing what they were doing, to trusting themselves, to becoming excellent?

That gap felt so intimidating to me. My confidence never seemed to match what I wanted to do with my life. How do I leap, when I don't know what I am doing?

Am I willing to suffer through the beginning stages of being terrible at something?

A stumble? Messy momentum? Tiny experiments compounding? I've come to conclusion: bad work is part of creating the good work. It isn't a detour from where I want to go. It's the path to it.

I know this now. So I intentionally put myself in new situations, so that I can learn to trust myself. I never know if what I make will go exactly as planned. I don't wait for my work to be flawless. I ask myself if it's true, and I move on.

At the end of the day, the work can be messy and dismissed, but tomorrow is another day to create again. As long as it feels honest, your integrity stays intact. No piece is a final verdict of who you are I are. .

All that say, there is no grand arrival. No waking up with clarity that it will all work out. There's only a place within you that begins to say, "I can handle the unknowns of this, because I have handled it before."

That's the process. That's all it ever is.
